**Subject:** All but one resolved — and you found a bug in our parser

Dear Jim,

Everything you sent back is now in the pipeline. Twenty-one of the twenty-two
questions are closed. Thank you — and particularly for the two answers that
were not the answers I asked for.

You spotted from the verbatim text alone that *Epipactis palustris* has a comma
after `(Crabronidae wasps)` where a semicolon belongs. That was not a taxonomic
question, it was our parser: the segment ran on, and eleven crabronid wasps
inherited Formicidae from the next family in the list. The same comma explains
*Pontia* on *Anacamptis morio* and *Lymire* on *Epidendrum amphistomum*. Three
of your twenty-two turned out to be one bug.

Fixing it corrected far more than the three records. Ninety-five taxa across the
database change family — fourteen skippers had been filed as swallowtails,
thirteen *Lasioglossum* as apidae, two hummingbirds had no family at all. None
lose a family. I would not have found it.

Your rejection of *Polia* was the other one. Had we trusted our own
family-consistency rule there, a pierid would have quietly become a noctuid.
*Pontia deplidice* stands as published.

Two small things, if you have a moment — no urgency on either.

**The one row you left blank** was *Tetralona nipponensis* on *Bletilla
striata* (Apidae), against *Tetralonia*. I suspect our workbook is why: the
evidence column compared *Tetralonia* with *Tetragona*, which is a stingless
bee and has nothing to do with the question. The actual choice is between
*Tetralonia* (15 records) and *Tetralona* as printed (1 record, from the
Japanese *Bletilla* study). Apologies for the muddle.

**Your *Lymire* answer opened a door I have not walked through.** "Old family
circumscriptions" applies equally to six other families in the database that I
did not ask about: Arctiidae, Danaidae, Satyridae, Eumenidae, Otitidae and
Coerebidae, between one and four records each. I have left every one of them
alone, because updating them is a judgement about how the compilation should
read rather than a correction, and that is yours to make, not mine. Say the
word and I will fold them forward; say nothing and they stay as published.

Where things now stand: the master workbook carries sixteen corrected cells,
each one a spelling you confirmed as an error, and each logged. Synonyms,
superseded families and the *Sp.* abbreviations are untouched in the source and
handled downstream instead, so the published text is still recoverable.

With thanks,
