**Subject:** Thirteen pollinator names we can't resolve — could you arbitrate?

Dear Jim,

A short ask, and I've tried to make it as short as possible before sending it.

While making the pollination records searchable by taxon, the parser flagged 155
pollinator names as possible transcription errors or misfiled genera. Rather than
send you all of them, I checked each one against the GBIF backbone. That settled
142. **Thirteen are left, and they need a taxonomist rather than a database.**

They fall into two groups.

**Ten spelling pairs where both forms are real genera consistent with their own
records**, so GBIF has no basis for choosing — *Vespula* against *Vespa*,
*Nomada* against *Nomia*, *Eristalis* against *Eristalinus*, *Scathophaga*
against *Scatophaga*, *Mycomya* against *Mycomyia*. Several are surely genuine
pairs rather than errors; that is exactly what I cannot tell.

**Three genera whose GBIF placement contradicts our records.** *Ectemnius* sits
under Formicidae in three of our records, where GBIF puts it in Crabronidae.
*Lymire* is recorded under Geometridae and Ctenuchidae, GBIF says Erebidae —
which may be a classification change rather than an error.

The attached workbook has a sheet for each of those, with the family as recorded, the
orchid records involved, and the verbatim text as published so you can see
whether a name was abbreviated after first mention. You type only in the yellow
columns.

There are two further sheets, and the third is the one I'd most like your eye on.

I accepted a GBIF match only when the family it returned agreed with the family
the record was published under, because several typos match a real animal
elsewhere in the tree — *Megasella* resolves to a brachiopod, *Scaria* to a
grasshopper, *Bombis* to a moth. Without that constraint they'd have been
accepted as valid names.

But that rule assumes the recorded family is right, and you know better than
anyone that orchids turn up odd visitors. So sheet 3 lists the **ten cases where
the rule discarded a spelling that is itself a real genus**. One is a brachiopod
and safe to dismiss. The other nine are insects — *Bombis* matching a geometrid,
*Scaria* a tetrigid grasshopper, *Pontia* a pierid butterfly against *Polia* a
noctuid — where "impossible" is too strong a word and I'd rather you told me than
have me assume. Two of them, *Thynoides* and *Zasipilothynnus*, look to me like
the Thynnidae/Tiphiidae split rather than typos at all, which is exactly the sort
of thing I'd get wrong.

Sheet 4 lists all 142 resolutions with the orchid records and the verbatim text,
in case anything else jumps out at you.

**Nothing has been changed.** The records still hold exactly what was published,
and will until you've looked.

Where you're unsure, please say so rather than guessing — an explicit "unsure"
tells us to go back to the primary publication, which is more use than a
plausible answer.

With thanks,
Ray
